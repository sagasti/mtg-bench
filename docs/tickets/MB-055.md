# MB-055: Spells with no legal target (counterspells on an empty stack) are offered at priority

- **Status:** done
- **Severity:** high
- **Area:** bridge / interface
- **Filed:** 2026-10-03

## Problem

Source: `runs/llm8-lsv-1` (gameplay-analysis review of mistral-small, verified). `InputDriver.playableNow` checks only whether a spell's cost can be paid (`ComputerUtilCost.canPayCost`), not whether it can be cast now with a legal target. Across the run, 659 priority prompts offered a counterspell (Miscalculation, Memory Lapse, Counterspell, Force of Negation, Mana Drain, Remand, Spell Pierce, …) while the stack was empty. Agents pick them, the cast fails, and the failures are charged as invalid answers or forfeits (mistral-small forfeited several decisions this way in r5). The legacy AI bridge filtered spells whose mandatory targets have no legal candidate; the human-controller path lost that filter.

## Suggested fix

- In `playableNow`, also require that the ability can be played now (`sa.canPlay()` / restrictions) and that each mandatory target has at least one legal candidate (`TargetRestrictions` / `sa.canTarget` over candidates), as the legacy bridge did; record withheld options as policy per DECISION-PROVENANCE.
- Add a conformance fixture with a counterspell in hand on an empty stack.
- Re-check the forfeit counts of affected seats; this inflated invalid answers for every model holding counterspells.

## Resolution

Root cause, two layers: `InputDriver.playableNow` checked only affordability, and the legacy bridge's
`hasRequiredTargets` (which MB-055 suggested reusing) didn't work for counterspells either: Forge's
`TargetRestrictions.hasCandidates` returns true for any target on the stack ("Stack Zone targets are considered
later"), so a counterspell always "had a candidate". The AI-controller bridge offered counterspells on an empty stack
too.

`Playability.hasRequiredTargets` (new `bridge/src/forge/bridge/Playability.java`) checks every targeting part of the
ability (sub-abilities included) with a minimum of one or more; for targets on the stack it counts the stack's own
spells and abilities (`getNumCandidates`), elsewhere it uses `hasCandidates`. It is used at priority
(`InputDriver.whyNotPlayable`: a card is offered when one of its playable abilities passes both the target check and
the affordability check), for a card's alternative abilities (`AgentControllerHuman.getAbilityToPlay`) and on the
AI-controller bridge (`PlayerControllerExternal` now delegates to it). Each card kept off is recorded as policy, once
per card, turn and reason: "card not offered: a required target has no legal candidate" (abilities: "ability not
offered: a required target has no legal candidate"). Modal spells (charms) are offered as before, since their targets
depend on the modes chosen; a card with another playable ability (Miscalculation's cycling) is still offered, for
that ability. Spells whose only legal targets are the caster's own permanents are offered: that judgement is the
agent's, and the target options say "your ..." / "opponent's ...".

Validation (2026-10-03, echo seats through the human bridge, same decks and seeds, master bridge vs this branch: two
fixed "stress" decks with 4x Counterspell / Force of Will / Black Lotus / Forth Eorlingas! / Fireball / Mind Twist /
Bitter Triumph..., and five LSV decks from `runs/llm8-lsv-1`; 29 games each): priority prompts offering a
counterspell on an empty stack 1,230 -> 8 (all 8 Miscalculation, offered for its cycling); "card withheld: its cast
just failed" 1,482 -> 384; cards kept off for having no legal target 929 (policy, counted). In `runs/llm8-lsv-1`
the same count (prompts with "Stack: Empty" listing a card whose rules text counters a target spell) is 888 of 5,558
priority prompts. No bridge errors in either set.

A conformance fixture with a counterspell on an empty stack wasn't added: the conformance decks are bot decks and the
check compares counts, so the existing fixtures plus the before/after runs above are the regression; the baseline
moved for this change (see `HANDOFF.md` §6, 2026-10-03).
